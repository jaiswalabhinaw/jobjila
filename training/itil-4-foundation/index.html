ITIL 4 Foundation

The vocabulary every IT service organisation runs on — and the fastest credential to hold.

Book the free first class Syllabus

ITIL is not a technology — it is the shared language IT service organisations use to describe how work flows. That is exactly why it appears in so many job descriptions: a person who understands incident, problem and change management can be useful in an IT team from their first week. It is also the shortest, cheapest credential on this page.

Syllabus

5 modules across 4 weeks, at about 4 hrs/week.

Service management as a discipline

What a service actually is, value co-creation, and the difference between outputs and outcomes — the framing the whole exam rests on.

The four dimensions and the service value system

Organisations and people, information and technology, partners and suppliers, value streams and processes — and how the SVS ties them together.

The seven guiding principles

Focus on value, start where you are, progress iteratively, collaborate, think holistically, keep it simple, optimise and automate — with real examples of each being violated.

The service value chain and key practices

Plan, improve, engage, design and transition, obtain and build, deliver and support — plus incident, problem, change enablement, service request and service desk practices in detail.

Question patterns, common traps, and two full timed mock exams with review of every wrong answer.

ITIL 4 Foundation — questions

Do I need an IT background for ITIL 4 Foundation?

No, and that is unusual for an IT certification. ITIL Foundation is about how service work is organised, not about technology. People from operations, support and even non-IT backgrounds complete it successfully and use it to move into IT service roles.

Is the ITIL exam fee included in the course fee?

No. The ITIL 4 Foundation exam is administered by PeopleCert and paid directly to them. We prepare you thoroughly and run timed mocks, but the exam voucher is a separate cost.

How hard is the ITIL 4 Foundation exam?

It is a 40-question multiple choice exam with a 65% pass mark and no prerequisites. It is one of the more approachable IT certifications — the difficulty is in precise terminology rather than technical depth, which is exactly what the mock exams train.

Is ITIL still relevant?

Yes, and particularly in India, where a very large share of IT employment is in service delivery and managed services. ITIL 4 explicitly incorporates agile and DevOps ways of working, so it is no longer the rigid process framework its older reputation suggests.
