ITIL 4 Foundation: what the exam is, and whether it is worth four weeks

ITIL 4 Foundation is the fastest recognised credential in Indian IT service delivery — and also the one people most often take for the wrong reason.

By Ms Puja, CEO and Organizer · 29 August 2026 · 8 min read

ITIL 4 Foundation turns up as a requirement in a large share of Indian service desk, delivery and support job descriptions. It is also the shortest of the credentials worth holding. Here is what the exam actually is, what it tests, and the case for and against spending four weeks on it.

The exam, in plain terms

ITIL 4 Foundation is administered by PeopleCert. The current format is 40 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes, closed book, with a pass mark of 65 per cent — 26 correct answers out of 40. Candidates sitting in a language that is not their first are typically allowed extra time.

It is a knowledge exam, not a practical one. There is no lab, no tool to configure and no scenario to design. It tests whether you understand the vocabulary and the model.

PeopleCert revises exam formats, fees and the syllabus version periodically. Confirm the current details on PeopleCert's own site before booking, and book your exam directly with an accredited body — a training provider should never be holding your exam fee.

What the syllabus covers

ITIL 4 is built around a service value system, and the exam works through its components:

  • Key concepts — value, services, products, outcomes, costs and risks, and the relationship between a service provider and a consumer.
  • The four dimensions — organisations and people, information and technology, partners and suppliers, and value streams and processes.
  • The guiding principles — seven of them, including start where you are, progress iteratively with feedback, and keep it simple and practical. These come up repeatedly.
  • The service value chain — plan, improve, engage, design and transition, obtain and build, deliver and support.
  • The practices — 34 in total, of which the exam covers a subset in detail: incident management, problem management, change enablement, service request management, service desk, service level management and continual improvement.

The distinction the exam keeps testing

More candidates lose marks on this than on anything else: the difference between an incident, a problem, a change and a service request.

An incident is an unplanned interruption to a service, and the goal is to restore service as quickly as possible — including with a workaround that does not fix anything. A problem is the underlying cause of one or more incidents, and the goal is to find and remove it. A change is any addition, modification or removal that could affect services. A service request is a routine, pre-approved thing a user asks for.

The trap is that the exam constructs scenarios where the intuitive answer is wrong. A user's laptop will not start: that is an incident, even though you might guess it needs a change. Twenty users report the same fault: still incidents, plus a problem behind them. Someone asks for access to a shared folder: that is a service request, not a change, because it is routine and pre-approved.

Get comfortable with those four and you have secured a meaningful chunk of the paper.

How long it takes

Four weeks at four to six hours a week is enough for most people, and that is roughly what a structured course runs to. Someone already working on a service desk can compress it further because half the vocabulary describes what they do daily.

What does not work is treating it as a memory exercise the weekend before. The guiding principles in particular are tested through application — you are given a situation and asked which principle applies — and that only comes from having thought about them.

Who should take it, and who should not

Worth it if:

  • You work on, or want to work on, a service desk or in IT support. ITIL vocabulary is the working language of those teams and appears in the job descriptions.
  • You are in delivery or operations at an IT services company. Client-facing service management roles frequently list it as a baseline expectation.
  • You are a student approaching campus placements. It is one of very few credentials achievable in a month that appears directly in real job descriptions.
  • You manage a support function and want a shared vocabulary for your team, rather than every person describing the same events differently.

Not worth it if you are a developer, a cloud engineer or a data professional with no service-management responsibility. It will not hurt, but four weeks spent on a technical certification will do more for you. We would rather say that than sell you a course you do not need.

ITIL is not a tool certification

A common and expensive misunderstanding: ITIL 4 Foundation does not teach you ServiceNow, Jira Service Management, Freshservice or any other product. It teaches the model those products implement.

That distinction matters when you read job descriptions. A posting asking for ITIL wants someone who understands service management. A posting asking for ServiceNow wants someone who can administer that platform. Many ask for both, and they are two separate pieces of learning — the certification gives you the first, and hands-on work in a tool gives you the second.

In short

  • 40 questions, 60 minutes, 65 per cent to pass, closed book, administered by PeopleCert.
  • Master the incident / problem / change / service request distinction — it carries the most marks.
  • Four weeks part-time is realistic. Memorising the weekend before is not.
  • It is a model certification, not a tool certification. ServiceNow is separate learning.

Questions people ask about this

What is the ITIL 4 Foundation pass mark?

65 per cent — 26 correct out of 40 questions, in 60 minutes, closed book. Confirm the current format with PeopleCert before booking, as exam parameters are occasionally revised.

Is ITIL 4 Foundation difficult?

It is one of the more approachable IT certifications, but it is not automatic. Most failures come from candidates memorising definitions without being able to apply the guiding principles or distinguish incidents, problems, changes and requests in a scenario.

Does ITIL 4 Foundation expire?

PeopleCert has introduced renewal requirements for ITIL certifications. Check the current policy on their site, as it has changed and depends on when your certificate was issued.

Is ITIL still relevant with DevOps and agile?

Yes, and ITIL 4 was specifically rewritten to sit alongside them rather than against them. Any organisation with users who raise tickets needs incident, problem and change management — the delivery methodology upstream does not remove that.

Do I need work experience to take ITIL 4 Foundation?

No. There is no prerequisite, which is part of why it works well for students and career changers. Experience makes the material land faster, but it is not required.
