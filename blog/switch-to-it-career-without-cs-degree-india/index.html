Switching into IT without a computer science degree

It is done constantly and it is not easy. The people who succeed pick one narrow entry point instead of trying to become generally employable in IT.

By Ms Puja, CEO and Organizer · 29 August 2026 · 9 min read

People move into IT from mechanical engineering, commerce, teaching, hospitality and BPO work every year. The route is well worn. What makes it hard is not the technical material — it is choosing narrowly enough, early enough.

The mistake that costs the most time

Trying to become employable in IT generally.

IT is not one job market. An infrastructure engineer, a data analyst, a service desk engineer and a developer share almost no day-to-day skills. Spreading six months across a bit of Python, a bit of cloud, a bit of SQL and a bit of networking produces someone who cannot pass an interview for any of them, because every interview is for a specific role.

Pick one entry point. Go narrow. Get in. Broaden afterwards from inside the industry, where it is dramatically easier — you will have colleagues to learn from, real systems to touch, and often an employer paying for your certification.

Which doors are actually open

These four entry points genuinely hire people without a computer science degree, and they suit different backgrounds:

Realistic entry points for career changers
Entry roleSuitsRoughly how long to get interview-ready
IT support / service deskAnyone methodical and comfortable with people under pressure3–6 months
Infrastructure / network supportPeople who like systems and troubleshooting6–9 months
Data analyst (reporting)Anyone already working with spreadsheets and numbers4–8 months
ITSM tool or process rolesPeople from operations, coordination or process work4–6 months

Development is deliberately absent from that table. It is not impossible without a degree — plenty of people do it — but it is the longest and most competitive route of the four, and it is rarely the fastest way into the industry for someone who needs an income.

What to learn, in order

Taking IT support as the most common entry point, because it also feeds the other three:

First, networking fundamentals. What an IP address and a subnet are, what DNS and DHCP do, how routing works at a basic level, and how to read ping and traceroute output and reason about it. Perhaps four to six weeks. Everything else assumes this.

Second, operating systems. Windows to a real administrative level, and enough Linux to navigate, read logs and manage a service. Another six to eight weeks.

Third, service management vocabulary. ITIL 4 Foundation, four weeks, and it appears directly in the job descriptions you will be applying to.

Fourth, one platform in depth — Active Directory, or a cloud platform. This is what turns a candidate into a hire rather than a maybe.

That is roughly six months part-time. It is not a shortcut, and anyone offering you one in six weeks is selling something.

Your existing career is worth more than you think

Career changers routinely present themselves as beginners with nothing to offer, which is both untrue and a weak position to interview from.

Someone from BPO or customer operations has handled difficult users under time pressure — that is the hardest part of a service desk job and the part employers cannot train. Someone from mechanical or civil engineering has systematic diagnostic instincts. A teacher can explain a complex thing to a person who does not understand it, which is most of what L1 support is. Someone from commerce or accounts already reads data critically and knows what a reconciliation is.

Name that explicitly. "I have spent three years resolving customer complaints where the customer was already angry, so a P1 with an escalating user is familiar territory — what I have added is the technical side" is a considerably stronger opening than apologising for your degree.

What to build

You have no work experience to point at, so you have to manufacture evidence. A home lab is the standard answer and it works:

  • Virtual machines on your own laptop — a Windows Server and a Linux server. Free, and enough for most of what follows.
  • A domain you set up yourself, with users, groups and a group policy that actually does something.
  • A basic network you configured, with segments and rules you can explain.
  • One cloud free-tier environment where you have deployed something and can walk through why it is built that way.
  • Written notes on three things you broke and fixed. This is the item interviewers respond to most, and the one almost nobody brings.

You are not trying to prove you are experienced. You are proving you can learn independently and diagnose problems — which is precisely what an employer is buying at entry level.

How long it honestly takes

Six to nine months of consistent part-time study to be interview-ready for a first support or infrastructure role, then one to three months of applying. Some people are faster. Very few are dramatically faster, and the ones who are usually had adjacent experience already.

The first role will probably pay less than you hoped, and possibly less than your current job. That is the cost of entry and it is temporary — the second role, with a year of real experience behind it, is where the maths changes.

The people who do not make it are almost never the ones who could not learn the material. They are the ones who kept changing direction at month four.

In short

  • Pick one narrow entry point. Trying to be generally employable in IT is the main way people waste a year.
  • Support, infrastructure, data reporting and ITSM roles are the four doors genuinely open to non-CS candidates.
  • Networking and operating systems first, then process certification, then one platform in depth.
  • Build a home lab and document three things you broke and fixed. Interviewers remember that.

Questions people ask about this

Can I get an IT job in India without a technical degree?

Yes, particularly in support, infrastructure, data reporting and service management roles. Some large employers filter on degree at the CV stage; managed service providers, mid-size firms and consultancies routinely hire on demonstrated skill.

How long does it take to switch to IT?

Six to nine months of consistent part-time study for a first support or infrastructure role, plus one to three months of applying. Longer for development. Anyone promising six weeks is describing a course, not an outcome.

Which IT role is easiest to enter without experience?

IT support or service desk, by a clear margin — the volume of openings is high and employers expect to train on specifics. It also feeds directly into infrastructure, cloud and service management once you are inside.

Do certifications help career changers?

More than they help experienced people, because you have nothing else on your CV for a screener to hold on to. One process certification plus one technical certification, backed by a home lab you can talk about, is the combination that works.

Am I too old to switch into IT?

Entry-level roles skew young and some employers do bias in practice. Prior professional experience is a genuine advantage in customer-facing and process roles, where maturity under pressure is worth something an employer cannot teach. Be prepared to start at entry level regardless of prior seniority.
