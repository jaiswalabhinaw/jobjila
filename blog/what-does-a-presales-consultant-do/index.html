What a presales consultant actually does, and why it pays well

One of the best-paid roles in Indian IT services, and one almost nobody plans for. Most presales consultants arrive by accident from a delivery team.

By Ms Puja, CEO and Organizer · 29 August 2026 · 8 min read

Presales sits between sales and delivery and is invisible from outside the industry, which is why so few people target it deliberately. It is also among the better-compensated non-managerial tracks in Indian IT services. This is what the job is.

The role in one sentence

A presales consultant works out what the customer actually needs, designs a solution that meets it, and makes the case that the firm can deliver it — before any contract is signed.

The salesperson owns the commercial relationship. The delivery team builds what is sold. Presales is the technical conscience in between: the person who has to say what it will genuinely take, to a room that would prefer a smaller number.

What the work is, concretely

Five recurring activities make up most of the job:

  • Discovery. Sitting with a prospective customer and finding out what the problem really is. Customers describe solutions, not problems — "we need a new ticketing tool" often turns out to mean "our escalations take four days and nobody owns them". Separating those two is the highest-value skill in the role.
  • Solution design. Turning the requirement into an architecture and a delivery approach: what gets built, what gets bought, how it is phased, what the risks are and how they are handled.
  • Effort estimation. Deciding how many people of what skill for how long. This is where presales consultants earn their reputation, because everyone finds out in six months whether the number was honest.
  • RFP and proposal response. Formal tenders, particularly for government and large enterprise work, arrive as long structured documents with a scoring model. Responding well is a specific craft — answering the question asked, addressing the evaluation criteria, and being explicit about what is not included.
  • Demonstrations and proofs of concept. Showing the thing working, to an audience that is a mix of technical evaluators and people who will decide on impressions.

Why it pays well

Because a presales consultant is one of the few people whose contribution is directly attached to revenue, and one of the few able to lose it.

A well-designed and honestly estimated solution wins the deal and delivers within budget. A badly estimated one either loses the bid or — considerably worse — wins it at a price the delivery team cannot meet, which turns into a loss-making project and an unhappy customer. Very few individual contributors have that much leverage over an outcome, and compensation reflects it.

The scarcity is also real. The role needs someone who can hold a technical design in their head and explain it to a non-technical buyer without either patronising them or losing them. Plenty of people have one of those abilities. Fewer have both.

Who moves into presales

Almost nobody starts here. The common routes are:

  • From delivery — a technical lead or architect who was repeatedly pulled into customer conversations because they explained things well, and eventually made it the job.
  • From support or infrastructure — someone with unusually strong communication who knows what actually breaks in production. This is an underrated route, because credibility about operational risk is hard to fake.
  • From sales — an account manager who went deep enough technically to design as well as sell. Less common, but very effective when it happens.
  • From consulting — advisory work and presales share most of their skills; the difference is largely who is paying.

If you are in delivery and enjoy the customer conversations more than the implementation, that is the signal. It is a preference worth acting on rather than tolerating.

The skills that separate good from adequate

Three, in order of how rare they are:

Saying no well. An adequate presales consultant agrees to everything in the requirement document. A good one says, in the meeting, that one item will not work as described, explains why, and proposes what will. Customers trust this far more than agreement, and it prevents the failure that starts after the contract is signed.

Estimating honestly under pressure. There is always someone in the room who wants the number lower. The ability to hold a defensible estimate — and to show the assumptions behind it so the conversation is about scope rather than optimism — is the core professional discipline of the role.

Writing clearly. A very large part of presales output is written: proposals, RFP responses, solution documents, executive summaries. Evaluators score what is on the page, not what you meant. Clear writing is a bigger differentiator here than in almost any other technical role.

What it is not

It is not a sales job with a technical title. You are not carrying a quota or cold-calling, though you will be in commercial conversations constantly.

It is not an architecture job either. You will design, but under time pressure, with incomplete information, for a customer you met last week — and you will hand it over rather than build it. People who need to see the thing through to production often find that genuinely unsatisfying, and it is worth knowing that about yourself before making the move.

In short

  • Presales works out what the customer needs, designs it, and estimates it honestly — before anything is signed.
  • It pays well because the estimate directly determines whether a deal is profitable.
  • Most people arrive from delivery, support or infrastructure. Almost nobody starts here.
  • The rarest skill is saying no well: naming what will not work, and proposing what will.

Questions people ask about this

Can a fresher become a presales consultant?

Rarely directly, because the role rests on knowing what delivery actually costs and what breaks. Two to four years in delivery, infrastructure or support first is the normal route, and it is the fastest one.

Is presales a technical or a sales role?

Technical, positioned in the commercial process. You design and estimate; you do not carry a quota. But you spend most of your time with customers, so pure technical depth without communication skill does not succeed here.

What is the difference between presales and solution architecture?

Considerable overlap. Presales designs before the contract, under time pressure and commercial constraint, and usually hands over. A solution architect typically works after the sale with more information and stays with the design through delivery.

Do I need domain knowledge for presales?

It helps enormously and it is learnable. Understanding how a bank, a hospital or a manufacturer actually runs makes discovery far more effective, because you can ask the question the customer did not think to answer.

What tools do presales consultants use?

Less specialised than you would expect. Documents, spreadsheets for estimation models, diagramming tools, and demo environments for whatever is being sold. The skill is in the thinking and the writing, not the tooling.
